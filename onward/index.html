Not a habit tracker

Onward holds a single thing in your life, something larger than yourself. A book you have been meaning to write. A relationship you owe time to. A body you want to rebuild. A practice you wish to deepen. A truth you have been postponing.

Name the thing. Make the vow. Walk.

It begins with your own words

Before you take a step, Onward asks what will trouble you on the way: the lie you will tell yourself when you want to quit, and what you will do when it comes.

On a hard day it does not cheer you on. It returns you to what you knew about yourself on the day you began. The app does not coach. It witnesses.

One step a day

Each day you hold one button to take your step, and the path is drawn behind you. At twelve places along the way, a resting place, a footbridge, a riverbank, you arrive somewhere and receive a small gift with a short story. One year, or two, or as long as you need.

An old word

There is a very old word for what Onward asks of you. Sankalp, from Sanskrit: the turning of your whole being toward one thing. Not a goal. Not a habit. A vow that reshapes the person making it.

The strength to keep walking does not come before the commitment. It comes because of it.

What we promise

  • No streaks, ever. A day you miss is never held against you. The path waits.
  • No subscription, ever. Walking is free for as long as you walk, and so is the first place with its gift. One purchase, made once, opens the eleven places after it, for this journey and every journey after.
  • Your words stay yours. Everything you write is encrypted on your phone. Onward has no account, no server and no tracking, and we never see a word.
  • One reminder at most. Onward sends one reminder a day, only if you ask for it, in your own words if you wish. No other notifications.
  • No ads. Not now, not later.
